CIRCUITLAB · TEACHER ACTIVITY GUIDE

45 minutes of hands-on discovery.

Cambridge AS & A Level Physics, Chapter 8.1: circuit symbols and diagrams. Students identify components, build complete circuits, use meters, compare series and parallel arrangements, and explain faults.

Open CircuitLab ↗

Prepare the classroom

Open CircuitLab on the classroom TV. Use landscape and fullscreen. Load Correct meter connections, run it, and check 0.3 A and 6 V. Turn on Teacher Mode and choose a three-minute timer. For competition, enter pair names in Classroom Challenge. Teams share the board and take turns while classmates predict on paper.

0–5 min · Notice and name

Open Symbol Explorer. Identify cell, battery, switch, lamp, resistor, ammeter and voltmeter. The long cell line is positive; an IEC resistor is rectangular. A junction dot joins wires. Crossing wires do not automatically connect. Use two identification questions.

5–10 min · Complete the path

Invite a student to place a source and lamp, then connect both sides. Predict before running. Remove a wire and explain why the lamp goes out. A source transfers energy to charges; sustained current needs a complete path. Charges are not used up.

Compare conventional current with electron flow. In the external circuit conventional current goes from + to −. Electron drift in metal goes the other way. Inside the source, energy transfer drives charge back towards higher potential. Arrow speed is illustrative.

10–17 min · Control and measure

Use Missions 2–4. Put a switch and ammeter in series, and a voltmeter in parallel across the lamp. At 6 V and 20 Ω, I = V/R = 0.3 A. Open the switch: sustained current stops. Move the voltmeter into series: its ideal infinite resistance blocks current. Discuss why an ammeter must never be connected directly across a real supply.

17–25 min · Series or parallel?

Load the series example: two 100 Ω resistors at 6 V have total resistance 200 Ω, current 0.030 A, and 3 V across each. In parallel, each has 6 V and 0.060 A; source current is 0.120 A and equivalent resistance is 50 Ω.

Ask students which quantity is shared in each arrangement. At a junction, current entering equals current leaving. A branch does not “use up” current. Try Missions 5 and 6.

25–32 min · Circuit Detective

  1. Series voltmeter: replace it with an ammeter in the main loop.
  2. Ammeter across source: remove that branch and put the meter in series with the load.
  3. Reverse LED: reconnect the anode towards + and cathode towards −, keeping the 330 Ω resistor. Rotation preserves existing wire attachments; rewire to reverse polarity. Expected current is about 12.69 mA.

Choose cases to fit the time available. Ask students to explain the fault before repairing it. Reveal the explanation after their attempt. An invalid circuit solution has no valid measurements.

32–40 min · Classroom Challenge

Assign Missions 7–10 to pairs and start the three-minute timer. Other students write predictions. Award 10 points for a validated circuit and a further manual award for a clear explanation. Scores are teacher-controlled. Pause stops timer and simulation. Next Mission keeps the board so students can extend it. Reset timer resets time; Reset scores clears points.

Ask: what happens when resistance increases? Why does an LED need a resistor? What happens to LDR resistance in brighter light? What calibration would convert resistance into a light level?

40–45 min · Exit ticket

Sketch a cell, switch and lamp with an ammeter in series and a voltmeter across the lamp. Label conventional current. Calculate:

  • 6 V across 200 Ω: I = 0.030 A.
  • 0.030 A for 60 s: Q = It = 1.8 C.
  • 6 V and 0.030 A: P = VI = 0.18 W.

Finish with one sentence distinguishing an open circuit from a short circuit.

Teach the model limits

This is a DC steady-state laboratory. Capacitor charging is not simulated. Lamps have fixed resistance here; real hot filaments have greater resistance. Sensor resistance is set directly, without temperature or light calibration. Diodes use simplified forward drops. Fuse overloads warn but do not automatically melt the fuse.
